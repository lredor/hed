# Domain model and editing rules

The source of truth is `backend/hed-model/src/main/resources/model/homeelectrical.ecore`.
Its namespace is `https://lredor.github.io/home-electrical-designer/1.0`.
The associated GenModel and Java model/edit code are regenerated by `tools/codegen`, using the `hed` base package.
Handwritten operations, validation, localized messages and samples live in `hed.domain`.

## Ownership and references

- A dwelling owns one local catalog, storeys, field equipment, distribution boards, circuits and functional controls. Its service connection is optional and can contain a meter and a service circuit-breaker.
- Storeys own rooms. A garage is normally a room, not a special storey type. A storey also owns the imported floor-plan image identifier and canvas dimensions; equipment positions remain representation data.
- Field equipment belongs to the dwelling and optionally references a room, a final circuit and a branch of that circuit. Changing or deleting a room does not delete its equipment.
- A distribution board owns its rails and modular devices. Each modular device must reference a rail from its own board. The rail's ordered device collection defines the physical sequence. Deleting a nonempty rail is rejected.
- Final circuits feed field equipment. Distribution circuits feed secondary boards. Source board and protection may be unknown while designing.
- A final circuit owns optional branches. A branch references existing equipment; it does not own or duplicate it.
- Control devices own one or more independent channels. Functional controls reference channels and target loads or outlets, with an optional modular actuator. Multiple controls can operate one load and one control can operate several loads.

Changing an assignment preserves the equipment object and its other references. Domain operations keep both sides of bidirectional references consistent.

## Catalog and names

Every equipment instance references a compatible catalog type. `DeviceKind` distinguishes outlets, control devices, loads, modular apparatus, meters and service circuit-breakers.

Modular devices inherit their current rating, power, width, residual-current rating/type, tripping curve and pole configuration from the catalog type. These values are not stored on each modular device. Domain accessors read them from the type; attempts to edit them on an instance are rejected. Editing a catalog specification affects all modular devices using that type and is subject to rail capacity validation.

For field equipment, meters and service circuit-breakers, technical defaults are copied from the type at creation and remain editable on the instance.

Business elements use `name` as their single identifier for display; there is no separate `reference` attribute. Names are free text and need not be unique. A modular device also has `labelText` for its printed label. The catalog retains `productReference` for the manufacturer's product reference. The inherited `description` remains available for general notes.

English is the default language; French domain messages and terminology are provided. Creating a new project initializes the catalog and its default type names in the creation language. Each type stores only one editable name. Changing the application language does not translate stored names or descriptions, including user edits. Equipment created from a type copies that type's current name, regardless of the application language; later catalog renaming does not rename existing equipment. New control channels receive localized default names at creation and retain them afterward. The empty and sample model factories both accept a creation language and default to English.

## Protection and circuits

`ModularDevice.upstreamServiceBreaker` links a device to the service circuit-breaker, whose `downstreamDevices` is the inverse reference. Alternatively, `upstreamRccb` links it to an RCCB on the same board. Both upstream references cannot be set at once, and RCCB chains must not form cycles. Directly downstream RCCB devices can be queried through domain operations.

A typical supply chain is service circuit-breaker, RCCB, MCB, final circuit, branches and equipment. A circuit has at most one overcurrent protective device: an MCB, RCBO or fuse from its source board. That device protects at most one circuit. An RCCB provides residual-current protection; overcurrent protection is represented separately.

Branches describe separate runs of the same final circuit under its common protection. Once a circuit has branches, equipment must be assigned through a branch; selecting that circuit directly is rejected. Assigning equipment to a branch also assigns its circuit. Selecting another circuit without branches clears an incompatible branch. Moving a branch to another final circuit updates its members' circuits and, when it becomes the destination's first branch, groups the destination's existing direct equipment. Rooms and functional controls are retained. Removing equipment from a branch clears its supply circuit. Deleting a branch retains its equipment: its members become unassigned if other branches remain, or keep direct circuit assignments when the last branch is deleted.

A distribution circuit links a source board to a target board. A board has at most one incoming distribution circuit, and board supplies must not form a cycle.

The model distinguishes the general supply phase from each circuit's phase assignment. It also records a circuit's usage and conductor section. Functional controls describe switching relationships independently of supply-circuit assignments.

## Rail order and capacity

Rails have an integer capacity in DIN modules, defaulting to 13. Device widths come from their catalog types; an unspecified width defaults to two modules for an RCCB/RCBO and one for other modular apparatus.

Creation and transfer operations reject a placement that exceeds the target rail's capacity. Reordering devices within a rail does not change their electrical connections. Moving a device between rails retains its board ownership; moving it to another board's rail is rejected. Rail order and board containment order are synchronized so a serialized model reconstructs the physical sequence.

Editing widths or rail capacities cannot increase existing overcapacity. An inconsistent draft can be repaired progressively as long as an edit does not worsen the overflow. Failed edits restore assignments, inverse collections and ordering.

## Values and validation

Unknown optional numeric values are null, not zero. Units are fixed: A, W, mA, kVA, mm² and DIN modules. Specified technical values must be finite and positive. Rail capacity and the number of control channels must be positive.

Structural errors reject domain edits and restore their previous state. They include references to another dwelling, incompatible catalog types, missing or cross-board rails, invalid protection, conflicting upstream assignments, supply cycles, shared overcurrent protection, inconsistent branch assignments and increased rail overcapacity.

Warnings allow an incomplete design: missing catalog type, room, circuit, source board, protective device, target board or incomplete functional control. The standard EMF validator exposes domain diagnostics as well as structural model constraints.

Deleting equipment removes incoming references. Deleting a protective device retains its circuits, now unprotected. Deleting a circuit retains its equipment, now without a circuit or branch. Used catalog types cannot be removed or recategorized. A control device must retain at least one channel. The dwelling and its catalog cannot be deleted through the ordinary element-deletion operation.

### References / Relations

Containment means that an object owns the referenced elements. Other references describe assignments or connections without moving their targets. Opposite references are kept consistent by EMF; the additional circuit/branch consistency rules are enforced by domain operations.

Le containment signifie qu'un objet contient et possède les éléments référencés. Les autres relations décrivent des affectations ou des connexions sans déplacer leur cible. ## Samples and scope

The sample model has two storeys, a garage, two boards, independent lighting/outlet/shutter circuits, a secondary-board feeder, a double switch and multiple controls for one luminaire. Branches are optional; the domain tests cover branch assignment, transfer and deletion. The tests also cover serialization, catalog specifications, protection, localized creation, ordering and rejected-edit rollback. Sample ratings illustrate data entry; they are not sizing recommendations.

There is no terminal-level wiring, conductor routing, electrical simulation or regulatory compliance engine in this version.
